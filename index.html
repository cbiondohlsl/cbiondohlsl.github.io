Legal Secondary Sources

Getting Started

Legal Dictionaries

  • Defines legal words, terms, and maxims.
  • Provides spelling and pronunciation guides.
  • Cites to relevant cases and statutes.
  • Examples: Black's Law Dictionary or Ballentine's Law Dictionary.

Legal Encyclopedias

  • Provides broad, brief overviews of legal topics.
  • Organized alphabetically by subject matter.
  • May be general or jurisdiction-specific.
  • Great for introducing unfamiliar areas of law.
  • Examples: American Jurisprudence, Corpus Juris Secundum, New York Jurisprudence.

Treatises

  • In-depth scholarly books on a single, broad legal area (e.g. - contracts).
  • May be single or multi-volume.
  • Written by legal experts or professors.
  • Analyzes case law, statutes, and trends.
  • Examples: Nimmer on Copyright, American Law of Zoning

Specialized Secondary Sources

Restatements of the Law

  • Compiled by the American Law Institute.
  • Codifies and organizes common law principles.
  • Includes helpful commentary and illustrations.
  • Frequently cited by state courts.

Legal Periodicals & Law Reviews

  • Articles published by law schools or bar groups.
  • Focuses on niche, novel, or developing law.
  • Contains heavy footnotes to primary authority.
  • Excellent for finding critical policy arguments.

American Law Reports (ALR)

  • Contains in-depth articles (annotations) analyzing specific, narrow legal topics.
  • Compares how different jurisdictions' courts rule on an issue.
  • Offers cross-references to primary law and other secondary sources.
  • Not available on every topic; does not cover every issue.

Looseleaf Services

  • Binder-format publications updated frequently.
  • Common in rapidly changing areas like tax law.
  • Combines primary sources, analysis, and news.
  • Provides the most current administrative rulings.

Form Books

  • Provides templates for legal drafting.
  • Includes sample complaints, contracts, and motions.
